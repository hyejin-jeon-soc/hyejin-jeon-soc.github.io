Instructor of record, Summer 2024 and Summer 2025.

This course introduces students to the fundamental statistical methods used in the social sciences. Rather than emphasizing complex mathematics, the course presents statistics as essential tools for understanding social phenomena. Students learn how to interpret and critically evaluate quantitative information, skills that are increasingly important in both academic and everyday contexts.

Topics covered include descriptive statistics, sampling distributions, hypothesis testing, statistical inference, correlation, and regression. Alongside these concepts, students engage with real-world questions such as: Who is included in a dataset and why? What claims are being made with the data? Do the numbers support the arguments?

The course also includes a lab component, where students gain hands-on experience using statistical software to analyze and interpret quantitative data. This practical training prepares students for more advanced statistical coursework and independent research, while also helping them become more informed consumers of data in the media and public discourse.

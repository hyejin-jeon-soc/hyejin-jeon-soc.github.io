I am a Ph.D. candidate in Sociology at the University of Maryland, College Park (expected May 2027) and a mixed-methods researcher who works with text and survey data. My research investigates the evolution of social narratives and attitudes across different groups and periods. By combining computational tools with traditional panel and survey data, I analyze how people discuss social issues in media and online spaces. I am particularly interested in integrating qualitative insights with quantitative analysis to better understand the mechanisms behind observed data. My current research focuses on the ways narratives of sex crimes, feminism, and singlehood are shaped and circulated within social institutions and everyday discourse.

My dissertation, "Can Rules Change the Narrative? Non-Judicial Policy and Gender in Korean Sex Crime Reporting," examines how South Korean news coverage of sexual violence has changed over time, and how policy shifts and digital feminist mobilization have reshaped that coverage. In related methodological work with Dr. Long Doan, I study whether large language model (LLM) classification of open-ended survey responses works consistently across response types and respondent groups, and what that means for the substantive conclusions drawn from survey data.

In addition to my research, I have taught Sociology 201: Introductory Statistics for Sociology and Sociology 202: Introduction to Research Methods in Sociology as a sole instructor, and served as a teaching assistant for advanced sociology courses across topics such as sociological theory, social psychology, and race.

During my doctoral program, I also worked as a data analysis intern at the Maryland Department of Health, where I contributed to program evaluation and compliance monitoring. This experience deepened my interest in how institutional practices intersect with public narratives to shape lived realities.

Prior to my Ph.D., I was a visiting researcher at the Korea Local Information Research & Development Institute (KLID) and the Korean Women's Development Institute (KWDI), contributing to policy research on gender and local governance.

I am currently on the 2026-27 academic job market.
